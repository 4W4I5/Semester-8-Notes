# Lecture 2: Design Thinking Toolkit
## **Introduction to Design Thinking**
Design Thinking is a **human-centered approach** to problem-solving that focuses on understanding users, challenging assumptions, redefining problems, and creating innovative solutions. It is an **iterative process** involving five key phases:

1. **Empathize** – Understanding user needs.
2. **Define** – Framing the right problem to solve.
3. **Ideate** – Generating creative solutions.
4. **Prototype** – Developing simple models to test ideas.
5. **Test** – Gathering user feedback for improvement.

These phases are **not linear** but rather an ongoing cycle of exploration, refinement, and validation.


## **1. Empathize: Understanding the User**

**Definition:**
Empathy is the **foundation** of human-centered design. The goal is to **deeply understand the users**—their behaviors, emotions, pain points, and needs.

- ### **Why Empathize?**
	- Design solutions **for users, not for yourself**.
	- Understanding users helps **uncover insights** that might not be immediately obvious.
	- Helps identify **hidden needs and expectations** that users may not explicitly state.
- ### **How to Empathize?**
	1. **Observation:**
	    - Watch how users behave in their **natural environment**.
	    - Look for contradictions between **what they say and what they do**.
	2. **Engagement (Interviews & Conversations):**
	    - Conduct **structured** and **unstructured** interviews.
	    - Ask **“Why?”** repeatedly to dig deeper into user motivations.
	    - Use storytelling techniques to **capture emotions and experiences**.
	3. **Experience the User’s Perspective:**
	    - Physically put yourself in the user's position (e.g., use a product the way they do).
	    - Shadow them to see their **real-world struggles**.
- ### **Key Takeaways:**
	- Gain **insights into user behavior**.
	- Avoid designing based on **assumptions**.
	- Empathy fuels the next stage: **Defining the Problem**.


## **2. Define: Framing the Problem**
**Definition:**
The **Define phase** is about bringing **clarity and focus** to the design process. It involves synthesizing all collected information into a **clear problem statement**.

- ### **Why Define?**
	- A well-defined problem **guides ideation effectively**.
	- Helps the team **stay focused on solving the right issue**.
	- Leads to solutions that are **meaningful and actionable**.
- ### **How to Define?**
	1. **Synthesize Research:**
	    - Organize findings from **interviews, observations, and user engagement**.
	    - Identify **patterns, recurring behaviors, and pain points**.
	2. **Create Personas & POV (Point of View):**
	    - Develop **user personas** based on research.
	    - A **good POV statement** includes:
	        - **User:** Who is your target audience?
	        - **Need:** What are they struggling with?
	        - **Insight:** What new perspective have you uncovered?
	3. **Ask “How Might We…” Questions:**
	    - Convert the problem into **opportunity-driven** statements.
	    - Example:
	        - Problem: "Users struggle with online banking apps."
	        - **HMW Question:** "How might we make banking apps more intuitive for elderly users?"
- ### **Key Takeaways:**
	- The Define phase **narrows the focus**.
	- A well-articulated problem **leads to better solutions**.
	- The next step is **brainstorming creative ideas (Ideation).**


## **3. Ideate: Generating Solutions**
**Definition:**
Ideation is the process of **generating a wide range of ideas** to address the defined problem. The goal is **quantity over quality**—**pushing beyond obvious solutions**.

- ### **Why Ideate?**
	- Encourages **out-of-the-box thinking**.
	- Helps identify **unexplored solutions**.
	- Builds a pool of ideas that can be **prototyped and tested**.
- ### **How to Ideate?**
	1. **Brainstorming:**
	    - Encourage **wild ideas**.
	    - Use **“Yes, and…”** instead of shutting ideas down.
	    - Aim for **quantity first, then refine later**.
	2. **Mind Mapping & Sketching:**
	    - Visually organize ideas to find **connections and patterns**.
	    - Use quick sketches to express thoughts.
	1. **Bodystorming (Acting Out Ideas):**
	    - Physically act out a scenario to **simulate the user experience**.
	    - Helps identify **pain points not obvious in discussion**.
	2. **Idea Selection (Voting & Ranking):**
	    - Use methods like dot voting to prioritize the most **viable and impactful ideas**.
	    - Pick **2-3 ideas** to move forward into prototyping.
- ### **Key Takeaways:**
	- **Don’t evaluate ideas immediately**—let creativity flow.
	- **Collaborate** to build upon different ideas.
	- Ideas should transition into **prototypes for testing.**

## **4. Prototype: Bringing Ideas to Life**
**Definition:**
A prototype is a **low-cost, scaled-down version** of an idea used for testing. It can be **physical, digital, or experiential**.

- ### **Why Prototype?**
	- Helps **visualize and test** ideas quickly.
	- Encourages **fast failure and iteration**.
	- Allows **real users** to interact with early-stage designs.
- ### **How to Prototype?**
	1. **Start Small & Simple:**
	    - Use **paper sketches, wireframes, or role-playing**.
	    - Focus on **core functionality** rather than aesthetics.
	2. **Build Multiple Prototypes:**
	    - Explore **different variations** before committing to one.
	    - Test different features separately.
	3. **User Interaction:**
	    - Let users **physically engage** with the prototype.
	    - **Observe reactions** and gather feedback.
	4. **Iterate Quickly:**
	    - Improve based on **user input**.
	    - Keep evolving the prototype **until it aligns with user needs**.

- ### **Key Takeaways:**
	- A prototype is meant for **testing, not perfection**.
	- Early feedback helps **avoid costly mistakes**.
	- Iteration is **key to refining** the solution.

## **5. Test: Refining Solutions**
**Definition:**
Testing involves **validating prototypes** by gathering user feedback. It is an **opportunity to learn, refine, and improve** the design.

- ### **Why Test?**
	- Ensures the solution **addresses real user needs**.
	- Provides **insights for further improvements**.
	- Helps refine the **problem statement if needed**.
- ### **How to Test?**
	1. **Observe Users in Real Scenarios:**
	    - Test in a **real-world setting** whenever possible.
	    - Watch for **unexpected behaviors and pain points**.
	2. **Encourage Honest Feedback:**
	    - Ask users **how they feel** about the prototype.
	    - Have them compare **multiple versions** to uncover preferences.
	1. **Iterate Based on Feedback:**
	    - Modify the design based on real **user insights**.
	    - Testing may **reveal new problems** requiring another iteration.

- ### **Key Takeaways:**
	- **Testing is about learning**, not just validating.
	- Be **open to changing your approach**.
	- The process loops back to **ideation and prototyping**.

## **Final Thoughts on Design Thinking**
- **Iterative Process:**
    - The design thinking process is **not linear**—you **revisit stages** as needed.
    - **Test > Refine > Repeat**.
- **User-Centric Approach:**
    - The best solutions come from **deep empathy and understanding**.
    - Constantly **engage with real users**.
- **Collaboration is Key:**
    - Work in **diverse teams** to **generate well-rounded solutions**.
    - Encourage **open discussions and feedback loops**.
- ### **Conclusion**
	- Design Thinking is a **powerful framework** for problem-solving.
	- The process **evolves over time**, adapting to user needs.
	- The key to success? **Empathize, iterate, and stay user-focused**.
