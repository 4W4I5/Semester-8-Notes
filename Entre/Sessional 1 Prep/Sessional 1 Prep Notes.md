# Lecture 2: Design Thinking Toolkit
## Introduction to Design Thinking

- **Definition:** A human-centered, iterative process for solving problems and fostering innovation.
- **Core Principle:** Put the customer at the heart of everything you do.
- **Key Phases:** Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test (with continuous iteration).

- ### Empathize
	- **Objective:**
	    Understand your users—their behaviors, emotions, and needs.
	- **Techniques:**
	    - **Interviews:**
	        - Ask open, general questions to get users talking.
	        - Deep dive into their hopes, fears, and challenges.
	    - **Shadowing & Observation:**
	        - Watch how users interact with their environment.
	        - Note discrepancies between what they say and do.
	    - **Engagement:**
	        - Have conversations that feel natural and unstructured.
	        - Listen non-judgmentally to uncover deeper insights.
	- **Key Insights:**
	    - Empathy reveals both the tangible and intangible aspects of user experiences.
	    - Capturing user stories and behaviors is critical for identifying meaningful design opportunities.
- ### Define
	- **Objective:**
	    Synthesize empathy findings to articulate a clear, human-centric problem statement.
	- **Approach:**
	    - **Data Synthesis:**
	        - Analyze interview notes, observations, and user feedback.
	        - Identify patterns and key pain points.
	    - **Personas & Problem Framing:**
	        - Develop personas to represent target users.
	        - Use "How Might We" questions to frame challenges.
	    - **Point-of-View Statement:**
	        - Combine user needs, insights, and challenges into a focused problem statement.
	- **Outcome:**
	    - A well-defined problem that guides ideation and solution development.
	    - Example challenges might include overcoming barriers for female labor force participation or developing eco-friendly product ideas.
- ### Ideate
	- **Objective:**
	    Generate a wide range of ideas and potential solutions without immediate judgment.
	- **Techniques:**
	    - **Brainstorming:**
	        - Encourage all ideas; use “Yes, and…” thinking to build on contributions.
	        - Emphasize quantity to fuel creativity before narrowing down.
	    - **Categorization:**
	        - Group ideas into themes (e.g., training programs, digital tools, advocacy).
	    - **Resource Assessment:**
	        - Consider activities, current capabilities, responsibilities, and additional needs.
	- **Key Points:**
	    - Creativity thrives when judgment is deferred.
	    - Ideation is about exploring diverse perspectives to uncover innovative solutions.
- ### Prototype
	- **Objective:**
	    Create inexpensive, scaled-down versions of your solution to explore ideas and gather feedback.
	- **Approach:**
	    - **Low-Fidelity Models:**
	        - Use mockups, storyboards, or simple physical models.
	        - Keep prototypes simple and focused on testing specific variables.
	    - **Rapid Iteration:**
	        - Embrace “fail fast” principles; build prototypes quickly and refine based on feedback.
	    - **User-Centric Design:**
	        - Design prototypes that users can interact with in realistic scenarios.
	- **Key Insights:**
	    - Prototypes serve as tangible conversation starters.
	    - They help uncover what works and what needs further refinement.
- ### Test
	- **Objective:**
	    Validate your prototypes and learn how well they address user needs.
	- **Techniques:**
	    - **User Feedback:**
	        - Engage users in realistic settings to interact with the prototype.
	        - Ask probing questions like “Why?” to understand underlying reasons behind user behaviors.
	    - **Observation:**
	        - Watch users as they engage with the prototype; note any unexpected interactions or challenges.
	    - **Comparison:**
	        - Test multiple prototypes to see which elements resonate best with users.
	- **Outcome:**
	    - Insights gathered during testing inform further iterations.
	    - Testing may lead to refinements in both the solution and the initial problem definition.
- ### Iteration & Integration
	- **Continuous Process:**
	    - The design thinking process is cyclical. Each phase informs the next.
	    - Iteration allows for continuous refinement and adaptation.
	- **Key Principles:**
	    - Remain flexible—adapt the process to fit the project’s unique needs.
	    - The true innovation potential lies in listening intently to user feedback and iterating accordingly.
