# Lecture 7a: Legal Aspects
## Law: Meaning and Purpose
- ### What is Law?
	- A set of rules enforceable in court.
	- Governs conduct of individuals in a society.
	- Varies by country, region, and system (e.g., federal vs. provincial laws).
- ### Purpose of Law
	- Provides security and social order.
	- Encourages justice, fairness, and equality.
	- Acts as a deterrent by enforcing consequences.
	- Regulates societal behavior and interactions.

## Types of Law
- ### Criminal Law
	- Focus: Offenses against the state.
	- Citizens have constitutional rights; state protects these by punishing violators.
	- Crimes include theft, assault, murder, cybercrime.
	- Punishment: Death, imprisonment, fines, discharge.
	- Police are responsible for crime prevention, investigation, and prosecution.
- ### Civil Law (Private Law)
	- Focus: Disputes between individuals or organizations.
	- Involves rights, duties, and liabilities.
	- Initiated by one party (litigation).
	- Subtypes:
	    - Law of Torts: Civil wrongs (negligence, defamation, trespass).
	    - Law of Contract: Enforceable agreements and their consequences.
	    - Law of Property: Ownership rights over land and goods.
	    - Law of Succession: Property transfer after death.
	    - Family Law: Rights and duties within families.
- ### Public Law
	- Concerned with state and citizen relationships.
	- Subtypes:
	    - Constitutional Law: Structures and powers of government organs.
	    - Administrative Law: Citizen rights against government agencies.
- ### Substantive vs Procedural Law
	- Substantive:
	    - Defines rights, duties, and liabilities (e.g., right to free speech)
	- Procedural:
	    - Mechanism of enforcing those rights (e.g., court procedures)
- ### Public International Law
	- Governs relationships between states.
	- Includes:
	    - Rules of war
	    - Boundary disputes
	    - Diplomatic immunity
	- States are subject to international law; individuals are typically not.

## Legal Structure of the State
- ### Branches of Government
	- Legislature:
	    - Law-making body (e.g., National Assembly, Senate)
	    - Enacts statute law
	- Executive:
	    - Enforces and administers laws
	    - Includes President, PM, ministers, bureaucrats
	- Judiciary:
	    - Interprets and enforces laws
	    - Independent from political control
	    - Makes case law through precedent

## Jurisdiction
- ### Types of Jurisdiction
	- Original: First hearing of a case.
	- Appellate: Hearing appeals from lower courts.
	- Territorial: Geographic scope of legal authority.
	- Pecuniary: Financial limits of a court’s competence.

## General Law vs. Special Law
- General Law:
    - Applies broadly to everyone.
    - E.g., Penal Code, Civil Procedure Code
- Special Law:
    - Applies to specific matters.
    - Overrides general law in case of conflict.
    - E.g., Cyber Laws, Consumer Protection Laws

## Sources of Law
- ### Common Law
	- Based on precedents and judicial decisions.
	- Unwritten but enforceable.
	- Also called case law.
- ### Statute Law
	- Written law passed by Parliament.
	- Formed through legislative processes:
	    - National Assembly
	    - Senate
	    - Provincial Assemblies

## Law in History
- Initially, the king made and enforced laws.
- Transitioned to democracy where laws are made by elected representatives.
- Modern legal systems distribute power among legislature, executive, and judiciary.

## Law and Ethics
- ### Relationship
	- Laws often reflect ethical principles.
	- Ethical norms can precede formal legal systems.
- ### Categories of Actions
	- Ethical and Legal (e.g., following contract terms)
	- Ethical but Illegal (e.g., whistleblowing in restricted scenarios)
	- Legal but Unethical (e.g., exploiting legal loopholes)
	- Unethical and Illegal (e.g., cybercrime)
- ### Examples
	- Legal and ethical: Firing an underperforming employee with cause.
	- Legal but unethical: Copying copyrighted software for backup when prohibited.
	- Illegal and unethical: Inserting malware, unauthorized data disclosure.

## Focus Areas in Computer Law
- Intellectual Property: Software patents, copyrights, trademarks.
- Computer Contracts: Validity of digital signatures and terms.
- Electronic Contracts: Online agreements, terms of service.
- Criminal Law:
    - Hacking
    - Virus/worm spreading
    - Unauthorized access
    - Identity theft
- Data Protection:
    - Legal frameworks for handling personal data
    - Consent, processing, storage, retention
- Privacy:
    - Legal rights to control personal info online

## Case Study: eBay v. Bidder’s Edge
- ### Summary
	- Bidder’s Edge used bots to crawl eBay's site and extract auction data.
	- eBay alleged unauthorized access and misuse of resources.
- ### Legal Allegations
	- Trespass to Chattels: Interference with eBay’s servers.
	- Trademark Dilution: Misuse of eBay's brand through misleading data display.
	- False Advertising: Violation under Lanham Act.
	- Computer Fraud and Abuse Act: Unauthorized access.
	- Misappropriation: Using eBay’s business model/data without permission.
	- Unfair Competition: Gaining commercial advantage unfairly.
	- Interference with Prospective Economic Advantage: Disrupting potential eBay transactions.
	- Unjust Enrichment: Benefiting from eBay’s infrastructure without cost.
	- Public Interest: Concerns around digital trespass and automation abuse.
